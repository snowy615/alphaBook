The book you run

You start with ${{ "{:,.0f}".format(start_equity) }} of equity and trade a basket of twelve names, each rebased to 100 on day one. Set a target position in shares for any name, long or short, and it fills at that day's close.

Two limits, and why they are there

Every episode in the library is a crash. If you could simply short the whole basket, the round would be a coin-flip on direction and nothing else. So:

  • Gross exposure — the sum of your position values, ignoring sign — must stay under ${{ "{:,.0f}".format(gross_limit) }}.
  • Net exposure — long value minus short value — must stay within ±${{ "{:,.0f}".format(net_limit) }}.

That forces a roughly market-neutral book. Your P&L comes from which names fall hardest and which hold up, not from the market falling.

What you know, and what you do not

Each name shows its published beta, fitted on historical crash data. That is your prior for how violently it should react. The episode re-draws each stock's beta around that number, and secretly assigns it to a shock group: defensive, normal, vulnerable or victim. A low-beta name can still be the worst thing you own.

Scoring

You are ranked on P&L minus {{ dd_penalty }} × your worst peak-to-trough drawdown. Making money is not enough. A round where you were down heavily before recovering scores below a flatter path to the same place.

At the bell

The round reveals every name's realised beta against its published beta, its shock group, and where the panic and rebound windows actually were. That is the part worth staying for.
