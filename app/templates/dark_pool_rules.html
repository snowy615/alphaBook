Dark Pool

Two hidden tiles, five shared ones revealed in three waves, real chips on every street. The twist: the moment you actually face a bet, the desk stops you. Before your fold, call, or raise buttons unlock, you price the spot yourself — a win-probability slider, a 90%-confidence range on that same number, and a chip-EV guess for calling. How a print works

1 · The deal

Every desk still solvent posts a flat ${{ ante }} listing fee into the pot, then gets two private tiles (your "book"). There's no button and no blinds — action always starts from seat 1, so there's no positional edge to play around, only the board and the betting.

2 · Four streets

Pre-Open (book only) → The Open (3 shared tiles) → The Revision (+1) → The Close (+1, five shared tiles total). A betting round follows every reveal. Best five-of-seven tiles wins the pot at showdown; ties split it.

3 · The calibration checkpoint

This is the part that isn't just a card game. Any time you face an actual bet — not a free check — the table pauses on you specifically:

ReadWhat it asks
Win probabilityWhat's the chance your book is ahead right now, 0–100%?
90% confidence rangeA low–high band you're 90% sure contains that same true number.
EV of callingIf you called this exact spot 100 times, average chip gain or loss?

Submit those three and your fold/call/raise buttons unlock — and you immediately see the engine's true probability and true EV next to your guess. Across the session the desk tracks your average read error, whether your confidence ranges actually captured the truth, and whether your own EV number and your actual action ever contradicted each other. That report — plus a couple of targeted follow-up questions on your worst misses — is what shows up when the session ends.

4 · The desk limit

A raise can never push the bet past the shortest stack still live in the print. Nobody is ever asked to call more than they can cover, so there's only ever one pot — the simplified price of not running side pots.
