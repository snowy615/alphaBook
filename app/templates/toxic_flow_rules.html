How a turn works

1 · Post the claim

Play 1–{{ max_cards }} cards face down and declare a rank. You post margin equal to cards × rank value, where 2–10 are face value, J/Q/K are 10 and an Ace is 11. Two Kings costs $20. Four 3s costs $12.

The first claim on an empty pile can be any rank. After that the table follows a sequence — each claim must be the next rank up, King wrapping round to Ace. Clearing the pile starts the sequence again.

2 · The {{ audit_seconds }}-second audit

Everyone else can stake money to look at your cards. One player can cover the margin alone, or several can pool until the stake reaches it. The moment the pool covers your margin, the cards are flipped.

Margin call

If your stack ever reaches $0 you are out on the spot, and your cards go back into the pile.

The end, and who actually wins

The hand ends the second someone plays their last card and survives the audit window. The bank then pays liquidity bonuses by how few cards each player is left holding — ${{ bonuses[0] }} / ${{ bonuses[1] }} / ${{ bonuses[2] }}, split on a tie. Emptying your hand is not winning: once the bonuses are paid, the biggest stack takes it.

House readings

Where the rulebook left room, this table plays it as: margin is priced off the rank you declare; the fine counts every card in the middle including the ones you just played; a pooled audit pays out pro rata with the largest contributor picking up the pile on a Squeeze.
