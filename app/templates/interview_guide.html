{{ question_minutes }}:00Stop and move on.
  • Start the clock once the question has been read out and any clarifying questions answered. The scoring view has a timer that calls out each step.
  • Checkpoints decide whether a hint is needed. Each question in the bank lists Hint 1, Hint 2, and the checkpoint each hint gets the candidate to. At 3:00, give Hint 1 only if they haven't reached Checkpoint 1; at 5:00, give Hint 2 only if they haven't reached Checkpoint 2. That's the whole test: not how confident they seem, or whether they ask.
  • No early hints. If a candidate asks before a hint is due, tell them the next one comes at its time if they still need it. Give each hint at the next natural pause after its time, within about 30 seconds, rather than cutting them off mid-sentence.
  • Only the official wording. Read Hint 1 and Hint 2 as written in the question bank. Don't give extra hints, rephrase them into bigger ones, or lead with questions that amount to a hint.
  • Clarification isn't a hint. Answering a question about wording or assumptions is allowed and doesn't affect the score.

Scoring rules

  • Hard-question gate. Move on to the hard question only if the easy question scored 3 or 4. With 0, 1 or 2, the hard question scores 0 automatically.
  • Adaptability is scored on the hints actually given. If the candidate solved both questions with no hint at all (easy 4 and hard 4), adaptability is 3 automatically: there was nothing to adapt to, and needing no help is the best possible outcome. If hints were given on only one question, score how they used those.
  • Score what they achieved in the time with the hints due, not whether they eventually reach the answer with extra guidance.
  • Likability and communication: most candidates should get 1. Keep 2 for genuinely stand-out cases.
  • Same structure and timing for every candidate, as closely as possible. Use the descriptions as guidelines alongside your overall impression of the interview.
